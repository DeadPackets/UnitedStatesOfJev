# Luna accuracy combos: which stack of levers, Rome 44 BC, Egypt 2012, Iran 1979

Model: openai/gpt-5.6-luna, reasoning effort none, latency sort, strict json_schema. 30 runs (6 conditions x Rome x2, Egypt x2, Iran x1). Packs in `out/combos/<cond>-<scenario>-<run>.json`. Runner: `combos.ts`; fetch: `fetch-combos.ts`; tables: `report-combos.ts`; checklists: `checklist.md` (Rome and Egypt unchanged, Iran frozen before the runs). Spend: $0.3502 on the 30 saved runs, $0.0178 on one smoke run that was overwritten, $0.0002 on the Iran plan call; $0.368 of the $2.00 budget.

Conditions: B = full sections, no levers. K1 = L4 (Wikidata dates and colors). K2 = L4 + L2 (facts sheet). K3 = L4 + L2 + L3r (reduced validators, one retry). K4 = L4 + L2 + L3r + L1c (code calendar). K5 = L2 + L3r + L1c (no Wikidata). Every condition, including B, gets lead plus the same full sections in the frame call.

## The recommended stack

**K4 (L4 + L2 + L3r + L1c) has the lowest error count: 1.20 per pack against 3.00 for B, for $0.0152 and 59 s.** It is the only condition that produced a zero-error pack on Egypt (K4-egypt-2) and it took Egypt from 4.5 errors to 0.5. On per-cent-of-cost it ranks second (1.18 errors per cent) to K1 (1.23), because K1 is almost free: $0.0001 and 2 s over B for 0.8 errors removed. K1 fixed Rome `leader_alive` (Pompey, Clodius, Crassus as leaders, 2 of 2) and Egypt `colors` (2 of 2) but added 2 Rome `regions_era` and 1 `no_real_member`. K1 also raised ref errors from 8.8 to 15.4 (patron wants/hates naming tags that are not in the tags list), which L3r's retry is what fixes: K4 has the lowest ref errors of any condition (2.6 against 8.8 for B). If cost is the constraint, ship K1; if error count is, ship K4. K3 (no code calendar) is dominated: same cost as K4, 2.00 errors, 5.0 ref errors.

The remaining K4 failures are two kinds. `ides_turn` failed in every Rome run of every condition (12 of 12): under L1c the model chose start_date -0044-02-22 (the first sheet event) in all 4 runs and the Ides are 22 days later, so no unit in {day, week, month, season} puts them on turn 16 to 18 (day gives 23, outside the term; week gives 4). The code kept the spec (model chooses start_date, code chooses the unit); had the code also set start_date = anchor minus 15 days, all 4 K4/K5 Rome runs would have landed the Ides on turn 16 with a day unit, scored offline from the saved dates with no model call. `dissolution` (Egypt, 1 of 2 K4 runs) and `no_real_member` (1 K4 Rome run, the surname Piso) are prompt-level misses no lever targets.

## What L4 adds inside the stack (K4 vs K5)

K4 beat K5 by 0.6 errors per pack (1.20 vs 1.80) at $0.0006 less cost and 4 s less time, so inside the stack the Wikidata table is free and removes errors. The whole difference is Egypt: K5 failed `colors` in 2 of 2 runs (the sheet has no party colors; the sources give none) and `morsi_seat` once, while K4 passed all of these. On Rome, K4 and K5 tie at 2.0. On Iran both fail the same item. K4 also halved ref errors against K5 (2.6 vs 4.6). The guarded lookup dropped 1 row per scenario (Cleopatra: no human item in the first 5 hits; Badie and Kianuri: no search hit) and moved 2 names to a later candidate (Octavian, Suleiman). The 150-year death-date guard did not remove the wrong Lepidus (d. 152 BC, 108 years before the start) or the 1996-born Octavian (no death date), so the guard as specified is too loose for Rome; a born-date guard, or a 100-year window, would drop both.

## Did Iran behave like Rome and Egypt

No. **Iran is the one scenario where the stack added an error**: B and K1 scored 0 of 11 items; K2 to K5 all failed `leader_alive` because the facts sheet listed Mosaddegh (from the National Front article) and the prompt says "use only people on the sheet", so 4 of 4 sheet runs made Mosaddegh (d. 1967) the National Front leader, where B and K1 named Karim Sanjabi. The sheet marked him `alive_on_start_date: false` with `died: null` in every run, and L3r only reads `died`, so the validator missed him 3 of 3 times; reading the alive flag is a one-line fix to test next. Everything else in Iran held across conditions: Khomeini and Bazargan placed, IRP, National Front and Tudeh present, the Revolutionary Council as the chamber in 6 of 6 packs, the embassy seizure as the dated event in 6 of 6, Iranian regions in 6 of 6 (one "Central Iran" in the smoke run only). Two Iran errors the frozen checklist does not count: Soleiman Mirza Eskandari (Tudeh founder, d. 1944) as Tudeh leader in 5 of 6 packs (all but B), and Khomeini leading two factions in K5. Rome and Egypt matched the ablation's pattern: the levers cut Egypt from 4.5 to 0.5 and Rome from 3.0 to 2.0, with `ides_turn` the floor.

## Method notes and deviations

- Sources per scenario: Rome 4 leads + 3 sections (21,273 chars), Egypt 7 + 3 (29,791), Iran 8 leads (Iranian Revolution, Interim Government of Iran (1979), Mehdi Bazargan, Ruhollah Khomeini, Council of the Islamic Revolution, Islamic Republican Party, National Front (Iran), Tudeh Party of Iran) + 3 sections of Iranian Revolution (Aftermath, Establishment of Islamic republic government, Hostage crisis) = 24,137 chars. Wikidata plan lists for Rome and Egypt were reused from the ablation (no new plan call); the lookups were redone with the guard into `sources/wikidata2-*.json`.
- L1c anchor rule (my choice, logged as a deviation): the facts sheet lists background and aftermath events (753 BC, AD 476, 2019), so the anchor is the latest full-dated sheet event within one year after start_date, else the storylet's date, else week. Unit = the one whose turn for the anchor is closest to 17 among turns <= 20, else season. Egypt anchored on 2013-06-28 (protests) in 4 of 4 runs, unit month; Iran on 1979-12-03 or 1980-01-12, unit month; Rome on the Ides, unit week (see above).
- L3r under L1c drops the crossCheck item "dated storylet has no turn" from the violation list, because the code computes that turn. The dated storylet keeps a `turn` field in the schema (the L1 schema in the ablation removed it, and that removal coincided with the underscore-tag regression).
- Facts-sheet dates for Rome came back as "-44-03-15" (two-digit year); the date parser accepts that and it compared correctly against the model's "-0044-02-22".
- The Rome `regions_era` regex fails paraphrases ("Veteran Colonies", "Spanish and frontier commands", "Military frontiers") that are not factual errors; 4 of the 5 regions_era failures in Table 1 are of this kind. The checklist was frozen, so they stand.
- One K4 Iran smoke run ($0.0178) was made before the batch to check the schema; the batch overwrote it. It found the "no turn" validator item above; it is the only pre-batch change to the runner.

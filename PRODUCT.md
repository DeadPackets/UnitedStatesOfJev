# Product

## Register

brand

## Users
People who like politics as sport: strategy-game players, news junkies, people who share Wordle scores. Laptop in the evening or phone on the couch. One session is one term, 10 to 40 bills, 15 to 40 minutes. They arrive curious about "the AI Senate", they stay for the 50-50 vote that went the wrong way.

## Product Purpose
Congress of Jev turns a fast decision model into a legible political game. The player writes a bill, sees 100 senators judge it in half a second, works the room, calls the vote, and lives with the headline. Success is a player who can explain why a senator flipped and comes back tomorrow for the daily chamber.

## Brand Personality
Civic, cinematic, dry. The voice of a wire-service editor, not a cable-news graphic. Drama comes from the numbers moving, never from decoration. Three words: composed, tense, witty.

## Anti-references
- Cable-news election graphics: saturated red and blue, flashing tickers, bevels.
- SaaS dashboards: identical stat cards, eyebrow labels on every section, gradient text.
- Editorial-magazine affectation: italic display serifs, drop caps, ruled columns.
- Casual mobile games: confetti, bouncy easing, badge spam.

## Design Principles
1. One thing to do next. Every screen has exactly one primary action, visibly hotter than the rest.
2. The chamber is the interface. The 100 seats carry the state; panels explain, they do not compete.
3. Numbers tick, never jump. A count that changes moves through its intermediate values.
4. Drama from data. Motion shows what changed and by how much; nothing animates for its own sake.
5. Playable blind and by keyboard. Party and vote are never carried by color alone.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.2 AA. Full keyboard play: every seat is focusable and announced with name, state, party, and probability. Live regions announce whip counts and vote results. Reduced motion swaps choreography for crossfades and skips the roll call. Party is encoded by position and text as well as hue; votes by filled versus hollow seats. Touch targets 44px. Sound is opt-in and never the only feedback.
